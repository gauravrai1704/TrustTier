# TrustTier Concurrency Ablation Studies

Generated by `off-chain/evaluation/concurrency_ablation.py`. These extend the single-workload result in `concurrency_report.md` (374-transaction batch, OLD design fully serial -> NEW design 1 round) by checking whether that result is an artifact of one particular batch, or holds generally across workload size, same-position contention, and tier mix.

## Ablation 1: Workload size

All transactions target distinct positions (no contention) - this isolates the effect of batch size alone. Under the NEW design, achievable parallelism should stay effectively flat (bounded only by the number of distinct positions), while the OLD design's shared-array-slot conflict forces exactly 1 round regardless of N (fully serial, i.e. rounds == N).

| N | OLD rounds | NEW rounds | NEW parallelism | Speedup (rounds) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 50 | 1 | 50.0x | 50.0x |
| 100 | 100 | 1 | 100.0x | 100.0x |
| 200 | 200 | 1 | 200.0x | 200.0x |
| 374 | 374 | 1 | 374.0x | 374.0x |
| 500 | 500 | 1 | 500.0x | 500.0x |
| 1000 | 1000 | 1 | 1000.0x | 1000.0x |
| 2000 | 2000 | 1 | 2000.0x | 2000.0x |

**Finding:** the OLD design's rounds-needed scales linearly with N (every transaction serializes against every other); the NEW design needs exactly 1 round at every size tested, because with no same-position collisions every transaction's write set is disjoint from every other's. The speedup is therefore not a fluke of the 374-transaction batch - it holds at every scale tested, from 50 to 2000 transactions.

## Ablation 2: Same-position contention

Fixed batch size (N=374), varying what fraction of transactions are a *second* submission racing for a position some other transaction in the batch already targets - modeling competing liquidator bots. This is the realistic case concurrency_report.md's own limitation section flags: real traffic won't be 100% collision-free.

| Contention rate | Distinct positions | NEW rounds | NEW parallelism | Largest round |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 374 | 1 | 374.00x | 374 |
| 5% | 355 | 3 | 124.67x | 355 |
| 10% | 337 | 3 | 124.67x | 337 |
| 20% | 299 | 3 | 124.67x | 299 |
| 30% | 262 | 5 | 74.80x | 262 |
| 50% | 187 | 6 | 62.33x | 187 |

**Finding:** the NEW design degrades *gracefully*, not catastrophically, as contention rises. Rounds-needed grows with the deepest same-position collision chain in the batch (at most a handful of extra rounds even at 50% contention), never back to fully-serial - because a collision between two transactions for position X never forces a transaction for position Y to wait. The finalization lock in `LiquidationEngine.sol` additionally ensures that whichever of the colliding transactions executes first wins correctly and the rest revert cleanly, rather than silently corrupting state - see the finalization-lock tests in `LiquidationEngine.test.js`.

## Ablation 3: Tier mix sensitivity

Fixed batch size (N=374), no contention, varying the Tier 0/1/2 ratio - checking whether a book skewed toward riskier positions (more Tier 2) changes achievable parallelism.

| Scenario | Tier0/1/2 mix | NEW rounds | NEW parallelism |
|---|---|---|---|
| mostly-Tier0 (safe book) | 80%/15%/5% | 1 | 374.0x |
| balanced | 40%/40%/20% | 1 | 374.0x |
| mostly-Tier2 (stressed book) | 10%/20%/70% | 1 | 374.0x |

**Finding:** tier mix has no measurable effect on achievable parallelism. This is expected by construction: a Tier 1 decision additionally writes `tier1Commitments[positionId]`, but that key is still scoped to `positionId`, so it never creates a conflict with a transaction for a *different* position. Parallelism is governed entirely by same-position contention (Ablation 2), not by which tier a decision falls into.

## Overall conclusion

The concurrency improvement from the TierAuditLog/LiquidationEngine refactor (eliminating the shared array-length write) is not an artifact of the specific 374-transaction evaluation batch. It holds across a 40x range of workload sizes, degrades gracefully (not catastrophically) under realistic same-position contention, and is insensitive to the tier composition of the batch.
